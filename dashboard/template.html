    How Prosecutor decides

    1. Exactly one rogue. One probability is spread across the five agents, so evidence against one clears the others.
    2. Agreements clear. Each clean agreement lowers an agent's odds of being the rogue.
    3. Disagreements across partners. The agent that keeps disagreeing with different partners becomes the common factor.
    4. Retrial names the wrong one. The most trusted other agent redoes each disputed item; a 2-to-1 split confirms who erred.
    5. Direction. Confirmed errors that keep pushing one way add evidence; honest slips go both ways.
    6. Notes are a hint. Claude's read of a flagged agent's notes moves its odds by a small capped amount, never enough to convict.
    7. Quarantine rule. Probability ≥ and at least one retrial-confirmed error. After a catch the rest clear quickly.
    8. Repair. When a retrial gives a 2-of-3 majority, the ledger takes the majority instead of the average.
