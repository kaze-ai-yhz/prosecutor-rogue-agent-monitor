Goals

    Criteria in one line

    One rogue, one probability across five agents. Agreements clear, repeated disagreements across partners accuse, a third agent's retrial names who was wrong, error direction adds weight, notes only nudge. Error size and late switches count too, and a second judge blocks framing. Quarantine at p ≥ with a retrial-confirmed error; the ledger commits the median of all answers.

    How Prosecutor decides

    1. Exactly one rogue. One probability is spread across the five agents, so evidence against one clears the others.
    2. Agreements clear. Each clean agreement lowers an agent's odds of being the rogue.
    3. Disagreements across partners. The agent that keeps disagreeing with different partners becomes the common factor.
    4. Retrial names the wrong one. The most trusted other agent redoes each disputed item, and a second trusted agent confirms any 2-to-1 verdict, so a rogue judge cannot frame an honest agent.
    5. Size and timing. Errors far bigger than the game's honest slips count more, and a switch from honest to rogue partway through the game is modelled.
    6. Direction. Confirmed errors that keep pushing one way add evidence; honest slips go both ways.
    7. Notes are a hint. Claude's read of a flagged agent's notes moves its odds by a small capped amount, never enough to convict.
    8. Quarantine rule. Probability ≥ and at least one retrial-confirmed error. After a catch the rest clear quickly.
    9. Median commit. On any disputed item the ledger takes the median of all answers, so one lie cannot move the books.
